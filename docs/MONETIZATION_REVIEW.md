## Separate the decisions

**Eligibility:** subscriber/watch-time/valid-Shorts-view metrics, program/country availability, account security, channel health and payment-account requirements are checked against the actual channel. No live binding or Earn-screen eligibility has been verified for either Ifverse profile. Do not infer a country from the chat timezone or invent subscriber/view totals.

**Originality / channel review:** YouTube's inauthentic-content concern includes mass-produced or repetitive content with little substantive variation. Reused-content review concerns repurposed material without sufficient original commentary, meaningful changes or entertainment/educational value. A consistent intro, font, narrator or series format need not mean the substantive videos are identical. Review the whole proposed channel catalog, not one clip. No upload-rate trick or automated originality score guarantees approval.

**Copyright / source use:** original commentary can be relevant to YPP and a legal exception analysis without automatically preventing Content ID claims or takedowns. Permission alone also does not guarantee YPP originality. A source hash establishes file identity, not ownership or a license. Calling a file a draft/review copy does not create permission, including if result files are accessible on GitHub. An exception such as fair use needs a case-specific, jurisdiction-sensitive assessment; a bot must not manufacture that conclusion.

**Ad suitability / disclosure:** assess the actual script, audible effects, visuals, title and thumbnail. Keep strong/repeated profanity and slurs out of the default style; mild clean wordplay still needs contextual review. A sound's friendly filename is not an audition. Evaluate YouTube's altered/synthetic disclosure criteria where realistic content could mislead; do not claim all AI narration automatically requires disclosure or that none ever does. Clear fan-fiction labeling and an accurate AI-assisted production description can help transparency, but do not grant monetization or copyright immunity.

## Our production standard (house practice, not an official YouTube formula)

1. Author an original premise, point of view, causal development and payoff. A comedy/satire story can provide entertainment value; every video need not become a lecture. Do not produce the same script by swapping character names or shuffle clips to simulate novelty.
2. Prepare a short [editorial brief](../prompts/editorial-brief.md). Explain what this episode adds, which facts are canon, which events/dialogue are invented, and how it differs substantively from recent videos. Preserve the actual script/version history without fabricating human authorship or review.
3. Design visuals to explain the specific idea: original diagrams, comparisons, licensed footage or other cleared material, with purposeful quoted excerpts only where the documented basis supports them. Original-looking fan art or AI art of protected characters is not automatically rights-free.
4. Review every footage, music and sound-effect asset. Keep the owner/source, permitted use/license or reviewed exception rationale, evidence link/document and restrictions. Replace or resolve unverified sources before marking publication clearance. The Brackeys public-domain library is a documented alternative to the unverified community meme recordings; it is a creative choice, not a substitute secretly applied to an approved video.
5. Keep Michael, the roast persona, captions and well-placed effects where they serve the story. Preserve intelligibility and accurate context; cosmetic edits are not the basis for approval.
6. A reviewer must actually watch/listen and assess meaning, captions/pronunciation, rights, disclosure, ad suitability and title/thumbnail. Channel-level topic/variation and actual Earn eligibility are separate checks. Keep alternate test cuts as drafts; do not upload every nearly identical engineering version as a new episode.
7. Keep publishing disabled until the required reviews, current channel/provider access and explicit publishing authorization exist. Neither the profile fields nor this guide implement a new automatic publisher gate. Existing technical tests remain technical tests; do not auto-mark editorial/rights/YPP approval from them.
