# YouTube policy fact check for Ifverse

Checked against live English YouTube Help articles on **2026-11-03** after `support.google.com` became accessible. The two owner-supplied texts mix useful advice with unsupported generalizations. This note supplements [MONETIZATION_REVIEW](MONETIZATION_REVIEW.md); it is not a YouTube decision, legal clearance or an executable publishing gate. No actual rejection notice or connected channel was supplied.

## What actually matters for our format

[YouTube's channel policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en) says each video's substance should be materially varied and provide creative, educational or other value. It allows a series following the same characters when each video has a distinct storyline, focus or concept. It lists repeated character situations with the same outcome, minimally varied story templates, and generic AI templates without original perspective as problems. Consistent branding, narrator, intro/outro or editing software is not itself the issue.

The same policy gives examples of potentially monetizable reused material: critical review, rewritten movie dialogue with a changed voiceover, and substantive editing that makes a meaningful difference. It also explicitly separates this review from copyright. These examples do not grant permission to download/reuse a movie or guarantee our channel's approval.

For Ifverse, keep `am_michael`, English, the dense playful roast style, purposeful captions and phrase-anchored effects. Develop episode-specific decisions and consequences. For example, Goku changing the Avengers' training, teamwork and strategy needs its own comic argument and payoff; substituting another hero into identical jokes and outcomes is insufficient. There is no official minimum number of new jokes, cuts or human editing hours.

Our new script and moving footage are useful evidence of production, but do not establish the whole channel's originality or the right to reuse each asset. The current Goku demo still has **ten footage assets and three sound recordings with unresolved reuse rights**, no recorded full listening/editorial approval and no verified Earn eligibility. This fact check changes none of those statuses.

## Corrections to the two supplied texts

| A few copyright claims snowball into strikes. | A Content ID claim does **not** automatically give a strike. A valid copyright removal request can. Claims can monetize, track or block a video; a dispute without a valid basis can escalate. Read the actual claim before choosing a response. [Claims](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6013276?hl=en) |
| Cartoons or toys mean mark it made for kids. | Assess intended audience and all relevant factors. Animation alone is insufficient; YouTube gives general-audience animation as an example that may be not made for kids. Mixed-audience content directed at children can still be made for kids. Do not automatically label all anime either way, or use edgy jokes as a labeling workaround. [Audience factors](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9528076?hl=en) |
| A robotic voice, no face or feeling fully automated automatically disqualifies a channel. | The verified policy targets generic/repetitive output and inadequate original contribution. It does not impose a blanket human-face or human-voice mandate. Preserve the chosen voice; assess the finished episodes and catalog honestly. [Channel policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en) |
| Avoid profanity only in the first 30 seconds. | There is no universal 30-second safe harbor in the fetched language table. It distinguishes severity, frequency, context, titles and thumbnails. Focal profanity throughout can limit ads; stronger profanity in titles/thumbnails and slurs have stricter treatment. Keep our occasional clean wordplay, and audition recordings as well as reading captions. A motherflicker substitution is no approval certificate. [Ad guidelines](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278?hl=en) |
| A yellow icon proves an algorithmic shadow ban. | YouTube's monetized-channel FAQ says monetization status is not used to determine how videos display. The underlying subject matter or policy issue can separately affect suitability or availability; a yellow icon alone does not establish suppressed recommendations. [FAQ](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9235997?hl=en) |
| Copyright permission or lots of transitions guarantees monetization. | Permission and originality are separate. A licensed compilation can still fail reused-content review; original commentary can still face copyright enforcement. Cosmetics and a source checksum resolve neither decision. [Channel policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en), [fair use](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9783148?hl=en) |
| Politics, religion, war or crime must always be strictly educational/documentary to earn ads. | Ad rules have category-specific context and restrictions. Do not turn this into a blanket rule about every mention of a topic. Review the actual sensitive subject, visuals, intent and metadata against the relevant category. A disclaimer or blur alone is insufficient. [Ad guidelines](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278?hl=en) |
| Inactive channels automatically lose ads; comments or a description edit prevent it. | The YPP page says YouTube **may** turn off monetization after six months without a video upload or Posts-tab post. It does not promise comment replies or description edits satisfy that condition. Regular uploads also do not override other policies. [YPP](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?hl=en) |
| Every demonetization disables all features, or bans accounts sharing an IP forever. | Limited ads on one video, copyright claims, YPP suspension, AdSense/payment issues and channel termination are different states. Serious violations can affect related accounts, but the supplied automatic IP/identity-blacklist narrative is not established by these sources. Follow the actual notice; do not create replacement channels to evade restrictions. [Disabled monetization](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1727191?hl=en) |
| There is a standard 30-day cleanup window, then a five-minute appeal. | There is no universal sequence. Scheduled YPP suspension can have a seven-day pre-suspension appeal window; a post-suspension appeal can have a 21-day window. An eligible video appeal must be **less than** five minutes. Follow the exact Studio deadline and route. The appeal page says **not to delete videos before submitting an appeal**. Remediation for reapplication is a different path. [Appeals](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9564590?hl=en) |

No unsupported promise about automatic reach penalties, income, successful appeals or permanently blacklisted banking details should become an agent instruction. Invalid engagement and policy evasion remain prohibited; do not buy views/subscribers, manufacture watch time or click our own ads.

## Eligibility observed on the official pages

These are general thresholds, not verified Ifverse account status. Check the actual channel's Earn tab and country/program availability when applying.

- **Full ad-revenue access:** 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 qualified public watch hours in the preceding 12 months or 10 million qualified public Shorts views in the preceding 90 days. Shorts Feed watch hours do not count toward the 4,000-hour route. See [YPP](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?hl=en).
- **Expanded early access in eligible countries:** 500 subscribers, three valid public uploads in the preceding 90 days, and either 3,000 qualified watch hours in the preceding 12 months or 3 million qualified Shorts views in the preceding 90 days. This gives access to specified fan-funding/Shopping features subject to their conditions, rather than automatically granting full ad sharing. See [expanded YPP](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13429240?hl=en).
- Other YPP prerequisites include compliance, an eligible country/region, no active Community Guidelines strikes for application, 2-Step Verification, advanced-features access, and an active linked AdSense for YouTube account or setting one up through Studio. Meeting metrics triggers review, not automatic acceptance. No channel country, security status, account link or metrics have been checked here.

The fetched pages still show these thresholds. They do not establish the pasted prediction about doubled thresholds on February 1, 2027. Keep that prediction unverified rather than encoding it as an announced rule.

## Make the requirements reviewable in our workflow

Use [the episode brief](../prompts/editorial-brief.md) before planning a commercial-publication candidate:

1. Write the episode's specific premise, causal development, commentary and payoff. Compare its substance with recent episodes; keep prototype variants separate from new episodes. A useful house exercise is to explain the episode's value without naming any editing effects.
2. Map each visual to the actual spoken beat and explain its purpose. Add original diagrams or comparisons where they clarify the argument. Keep canon, hypothetical events and invented dialogue distinct. Do not claim illustrative source footage literally depicts the invented timeline.
3. Record a real source-use basis and evidence per footage/music/effect asset: permission/license scope and restrictions, ownership, or a genuinely reviewed case-specific exception rationale. For borrowed fictional footage, consider all applicable exception factors; short duration, attribution, a fan label or jokes alone are insufficient. Do not fabricate a license or an automated fair-use verdict. Public-domain effects in the existing Brackeys catalog are an explicit alternative; do not silently replace the owner's selected recordings.
4. Watch and listen to the finished file, including downloaded effects. Review meaning, pronunciation, caption timing, violent/sexual imagery, actual audience factors, misleading metadata and applicable AI disclosure. [The disclosure page](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en) says realistic, meaningfully altered content can require disclosure, and disclosure alone does not reduce monetization eligibility. It is not a blanket exemption for every use of synthetic speech.
5. Preserve actual script/plan versions, source evidence, timeline/cue receipts and final hashes. Record real reviewer decisions with unresolved items left pending. A production log helps explain how a video was made; it does not replace original value or rights.
6. Review the proposed channel catalog as well as the individual file. YouTube may examine the main theme, newest/most-viewed videos, largest watch-time sources, metadata and About section. Accurate channel descriptions can explain our authored hypothetical comedy and AI-assisted production without claiming human-recorded speech or cleared assets that have not been established.

These are planning/review instructions. No executable monetization gate has been added. Publishing remains disabled; no video was uploaded, removed or appealed by this update.
