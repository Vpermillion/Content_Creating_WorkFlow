# Ifverse script style

Owner-confirmed preference **ifverse-playful-roast-v2**, clarified 2026-10-30. Applies to English `ifverse_anime` and `ifverse_hollywood` scripts unless the user changes the direction. Default voice: `am_michael`.

## Narrator and story

Keep the narrator funny, cheeky, creatively annoyed and casually streetwise throughout the story. Use frequent, varied roasts, playful trash talk, Gen Z/millennial banter, exaggerated complaints, mock praise, specific comparisons and callbacks. Make the jokes explain that character's decision, bad plan or absurd consequence. One isolated insult or the same catchphrase repeated is insufficient.

Open with the what-if and a funny reaction. Build a clear alternate timeline: setup, cause, escalation, consequence and payoff. Keep the comic persona between punchlines, with sincere stakes where useful. End with a punchline or concrete audience question. Avoid dry summaries, generic filler and repeated engagement requests.

Slang such as “bro,” “my guy,” “cooked,” “side quest” and “group chat” is optional vocabulary, not a checklist. Vary the phrasing. Colloquial swagger does not require a racial stereotype or imitation of a real person's voice.

**Roasts are frequent; swearing is occasional.** Prefer mild wording or clean substitutions such as “motherflicker,” “mother father” or “what the heck” when the joke earns it. Preserve that wording in narration and captions. Avoid slurs and explicit sexual language.

## Examples of comic moves

- Mock career advice: “Bro saw a villain cloak and called it career growth.”
- Reversal: “You are not the new hire. You are the delivery order.”
- Specific comparison: “Akatsuki is a group project where everybody brought a criminal record.”
- Mock praise: “Outstanding decision. Somehow both teams want your location now.”
- Callback: set up the cloak as a career upgrade, then close with “Keep the cloak. Cancel the career advice.”

These are illustrative fan-commentary examples. Write fresh jokes and episode-specific development rather than copying them into each script.

## Production notes

- Distinguish the counterfactual from canon and invented dialogue from official quotes.
- Write filmable beats with concrete characters, actions and places. Record shot reasons; a metaphor does not justify unrelated footage.
- Measure the generated narration for timing. Align captions to the final waveform; retain matching speech/captions for visual-only revisions.
- Put sound directions in the cue plan rather than spoken text. Select short effects for specific reactions, anchor them to spoken phrases and keep speech intelligible. See [sound effects](../docs/SOUND_EFFECTS.md).
- Use [the episode brief](editorial-brief.md) and [review guidance](../docs/MONETIZATION_REVIEW.md) when preparing a publication candidate. Repeated branding and a consistent voice can stay; the story, reasoning and jokes should be its own.
