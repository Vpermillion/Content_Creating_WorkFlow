# Ifverse script-style addon — owner preference

Applies by default to English scripts for **ifverse_anime** and **ifverse_hollywood**. Confirmed by the owner on 2026-10-30. Preserve this preference across sessions; this file supersedes the older neutral/explainer tone. The owner may override it for an individual video.

## Copy-ready writing prompt

Write like an entertaining, exasperated fan roasting a ridiculous fictional decision. Be funny, teasing, conversational and confidently sarcastic. Use exaggerated complaints, short reactions, playful insults toward fictional characters, modern internet phrasing and a strong point of view. Examples: “Brother, read the stupid contract,” “Congratulations, genius,” or “This group project is a disaster.” Keep it natural rather than stuffing every sentence with slang. Use a general lively streetwise/internet-comedy cadence without racial impersonation or stereotypes.

Open with the what-if and an immediate funny reaction. Build a coherent alternate timeline with cause, escalation, a consequence and a choice. Make jokes specific to the character or scene. Mix punchlines with enough sincere stakes for the story to matter. End with a punchline or concrete audience question. Avoid a dry encyclopedia voice, generic motivational filler and repeated engagement requests.

Occasional mild swearing is welcome when it earns the joke. Prefer playful clean substitutions such as **“motherflicker,” “mother father,” “what the heck,”** or **“holy crap.”** A sensible default is zero to two clean-swear punchlines per minute, not a quota; do not put one in every sentence. Do not silently restore the stronger phrase in TTS, captions, titles or descriptions. Keep profanity light; avoid explicit sexual language and slurs. Clean substitutions do not guarantee platform acceptance, monetization or freedom from moderation.

Aim the roast at fictional behavior, bad plans, villains and plot absurdity. Keep speculation clearly distinct from canon: introduce an alternate timeline, do not invent official quotes or assert that an imagined event appeared in the actual show/film. No particular actor, real person's voice, accent or racial identity is requested by this style preference.

Write **filmable narration beats**. Name concrete characters, objects, actions or places that the available moving footage can illustrate. Give each planned beat its source and match reason; preserve correct named characters when a joke lands. Metaphors like “job interview” do not justify unrelated office stock footage in an anime story. Illustrate the character/villain context and label hypothetical actions honestly.

For an approximately one-minute video, draft around 170–200 spoken English words, then measure actual narration. Allow breathing room and comic timing. The current 60-second example uses pitch-preserving bounded speech retiming; captions must be generated/aligned to the final waveform. Never reuse another script's narration or timestamps. Do not promise an expressive performance the chosen TTS has not demonstrated.
